## Purpose

Competitor research exists to improve Kendrix's decisions.

It does not exist to imitate competitors.

We study the market to better understand creators, identify opportunities, and validate or challenge our own assumptions.

Our goal is not to become the best version of another product.

Our goal is to become the best version of Kendrix.

---

## Research Philosophy

### Learn Before Judging

Assume every successful product is solving a real problem.

Understand that problem before evaluating the solution.

### Study Strategy, Not Features

Features are visible.

Strategy is not.

When researching a competitor, ask:

* Who are they serving?
* What problem are they solving?
* Why does their product look the way it does?

Feature comparisons without strategic understanding are shallow.

### Context Matters

A decision that is correct for another company may be wrong for Kendrix.

Always evaluate decisions within the context of:

* their customer
* their business model
* their stage
* their constraints

### Positioning Over Parity

We do not build features simply because competitors have them.

Every proposed feature should strengthen Kendrix's positioning.

If a competitor's feature weakens our identity, we intentionally reject it.

### Trends Are Signals, Not Instructions

Market trends deserve attention.

They do not deserve automatic adoption.

A trend becomes relevant only if it serves our creators and aligns with our principles.

### Evidence Over Opinion

Avoid statements such as:

"Everyone is doing this."

Instead ask:

* What evidence supports it?
* Who benefits?
* Does it align with our positioning?
* Does it solve a meaningful creator problem?

## Categories

### Direct Competitors

Products solving a similar creator problem.

Examples:

* AI storytelling platforms
* AI video creation platforms
* faceless content tools

### Adjacent Products

Products serving similar creators through different workflows.

Examples:

* video editors
* design tools
* writing tools
* publishing platforms

### Inspirational Companies

Companies we study because they demonstrate exceptional thinking.

Examples may include:

* workflow design
* product simplicity
* customer experience
* pricing
* community
* onboarding

Inspiration does not imply imitation.

## Evaluation Framework

Every significant competitor should be evaluated using the same framework.

Individual evaluations live as separate files in research/ (one per competitor). This document is the lens, not the archive.

### Overview

* Product
* Company
* Stage
* Target Customer

### Positioning

How do they describe themselves?

What promise are they making?

### Workflow

How does work flow through the product?

What assumptions are built into the workflow?

### Storytelling Philosophy

How much emphasis is placed on storytelling quality versus production speed?

### AI Philosophy

How visible is AI to the customer?

Is AI presented as the product or as an implementation detail?

### Business Model

* pricing
* monetization
* free experience
* paid experience

### Strengths

What do they consistently do well?

### Weaknesses

Where does the experience break down?

### Lessons

What should Kendrix learn?

### What We Adopt

Ideas that strengthen Kendrix.

Record the reasoning.

Adoption is a proposal, not a decision. Every adopted idea still enters the decision framework at Step 1 — an observed creator problem — like any other idea.

### What We Reject

Ideas that conflict with Kendrix's principles.

Record the reasoning.

## Market Signals

Record recurring patterns observed across multiple competitors.

Examples:

* common customer pain points
* pricing patterns
* workflow expectations
* creator complaints

One competitor is an anecdote.

Repeated patterns are evidence.

---

## Opportunities

Problems competitors are not solving.

White space is where positioning lives — the most valuable output of this lens is the creator problem nobody else is taking seriously.

Record each opportunity with the evidence that it exists and the assumption it would test.

## Relationship to Assumptions

Competitor research may:

* strengthen assumptions
* challenge assumptions
* create new assumptions

It should not replace direct evidence from Kendrix's own creators.

---

## Relationship to Customer Zero

Customer Zero remains Kendrix's primary source of learning.

Competitor research provides context.

Customer Zero provides evidence.

When they disagree, investigate.

Do not automatically trust competitors over your own observations.

---

## Review Questions

* What changed in the market?
* Which assumptions are being challenged?
* Are competitors solving new creator problems?
* Has Kendrix's differentiation become stronger or weaker?
* Are we reacting to competitors or serving creators?

---

## Definition of Success

Competitor research succeeds when it improves Kendrix's strategic decisions.

If research causes Kendrix to imitate competitors instead of strengthening its own identity, the research has failed.
