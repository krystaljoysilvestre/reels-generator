# Quality Gates

## Purpose

Kendrix should encourage quality before production.

Each stage has quality expectations.

Progressing to the next stage without meeting them should be discouraged for creators — and refused for the machine.

---

## Creative Brief Gate

Questions:

* Is the objective clear?
* Is the audience defined?
* Is the genre selected?
* Is the emotional goal understood?

---

## Story Gate

Checklist:

* Strong opening
* Clear narrative progression
* Appropriate pacing
* Logical flow
* Satisfying ending
* Genre consistency
* No unnecessary repetition

Reject stories that feel generic or directionless.

The definition of good — and the failure modes judged first — lives in storytelling-philosophy.md. This checklist is the per-stage minimum, not the full criteria.

---

## Storyboard Gate

Checklist:

* Every scene has purpose.
* Scene order is logical.
* Emotional progression exists.
* Visual intent is clear.
* Scene transitions make sense.

---

## Visual Gate

Checklist:

* Character consistency
* Environmental consistency
* Appropriate composition
* Genre-appropriate style
* Visual continuity

---

## Narration Gate

Checklist:

* Natural pacing
* Correct pronunciation
* Emotional delivery
* Clear timing
* No distracting repetition

---

## Audio Gate

Checklist:

* Music supports emotion
* Sound effects support action
* Audio balance is appropriate
* Silence is used intentionally

Avoid unnecessary noise.

---

## Final Review Gate

Before publishing:

* Story is engaging.
* Visuals are consistent.
* Audio is synchronized.
* Captions are accurate.
* Branding is correct.
* Overall quality reflects Kendrix standards.

---

## Limits of Judgment

A gate can only check what it can verify.

Factual truth cannot be self-checked. A gate judging facts against its own knowledge will approve myths and hallucinate corrections. Factual claims require external grounding.

Gate criteria are themselves audited. A gate that rewards surface patterns ("short punchy lines") manufactures machine monoculture — the same anti-prescription rule from the storytelling philosophy applies to what gates reward.

---

## Philosophy

Quality gates face two directions.

**Toward the machine, gates are judges.** They reject work that fails the criteria, and the system regenerates it. Guidance does not produce quality; judgment does. A rejected story is regenerated — bounded, then escalated to a human as an exception. The system never publishes a failing story and never loops forever. The creator usually never sees any of this.

**Toward the creator, gates are guides.** They educate rather than block, explain why a recommendation exists, and increase confidence rather than create friction. Friction is for the machine, not the creator.

Gates judge; they do not create. A gate may reject and name the failure, but the fix is always regenerated by the creative layer — a gate never writes the correction itself.

Current enforcement tactics (pass-rate thresholds, regeneration caps) live in the minimal-review gate policy, not here. This document owns the philosophy; tactics are tuned by evidence.
