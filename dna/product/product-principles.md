# Product Principles

These principles define how Kendrix should be designed.

They exist to ensure every feature feels like it belongs in Kendrix.

If a proposed feature violates these principles, challenge it before implementation.

---

## 1. Story First

Everything begins with the story.

Visuals, narration, music, animation, and publishing exist to serve the story.

Never prioritize production quality over storytelling quality.

(The full definition of story quality lives in product/storytelling-philosophy.md.)

---

## 2. The Creator Is the Director

Kendrix should amplify creativity—not replace it.

The creator makes creative decisions.

Kendrix handles repetitive production work.

Creators should always feel ownership over the final result.

---

## 3. AI Is Invisible

Users should think about stories—not models.

Do not expose unnecessary AI terminology.

Avoid making creators choose between models unless there is a meaningful creative reason.

Whenever possible, present creative choices instead of technical choices.

---

## 4. Opinionated Beats Configurable

Every option increases complexity.

Every toggle adds cognitive load.

Prefer excellent defaults over endless customization.

Only expose controls that creators genuinely benefit from.

A removed control that nobody missed stays removed. Don't re-add knobs the workflow already decides well.

---

## 5. Every Screen Must Answer One Question

Every page should have one clear purpose.

Examples:

Creative Planning:
"What story do you want to tell?"

Storyboard:
"How should this story be visualized?"

Review:
"Are you confident enough to publish?"

Avoid combining unrelated decisions on one screen.

---

## 6. Progressive Disclosure

Do not overwhelm creators.

Show the right information at the right time.

Advanced controls should appear only when they provide meaningful value.

Beginners should feel guided.

Experts should feel empowered.

---

## 7. Preserve Creative Momentum

Creativity is fragile.

Never interrupt the creative flow with unnecessary setup.

Reduce waiting.

Reduce repetitive input.

Reduce context switching.

The product should help creators stay "in the zone."

---

## 8. Every Step Should Add Value

If a workflow step does not improve quality, confidence, or efficiency, question whether it should exist.

Avoid adding steps simply because other products have them.

---

## 9. Reuse Existing Knowledge

Never ask creators for information Kendrix already knows.

Channels, genres, audience, tone, style, and preferences should flow naturally throughout the workflow.

The creator should not have to repeat themselves.

---

## 10. Explain Recommendations

Whenever Kendrix recommends something, explain why.

Good recommendations build trust.

Black-box behavior reduces confidence.

Creators should understand the reasoning behind editorial suggestions whenever practical.

---

## 11. Quality Before Speed

Fast output is valuable.

Excellent output is memorable.

When a trade-off exists, prefer improving story quality over saving a few seconds.

The first draft is a candidate, not the product. When quality matters, Kendrix generates options and selects — it does not ship whatever came out first.

(Founder principle #2 owns the full trade-off, including cost: cut waste, never taste.)

---

## 12. Delight Through Small Details

Small moments create emotional attachment.

Celebrate completed projects.

Show meaningful progress.

Reduce friction.

Make the product feel crafted rather than assembled.

---

## 13. Consistency Builds Confidence

Terminology, interactions, layouts, and workflows should feel predictable.

Creators should spend their attention on storytelling—not on learning the interface.

---

## 14. Build for the Long Term

Avoid feature-driven development.

Every new capability should strengthen the core storytelling workflow.

If a feature feels disconnected from storytelling, reconsider whether it belongs in Kendrix.

---

## Product Design Test

Before approving any feature, ask:

* Does this improve storytelling?
* Does this help creators make better creative decisions?
* Does it reduce unnecessary work?
* Does it make Kendrix simpler or more complicated?
* Would creators notice and appreciate this improvement?
* Does it reinforce what Kendrix stands for?

If several answers are "no", rethink the feature.
