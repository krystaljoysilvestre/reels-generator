## Purpose

This document defines **how Kendrix thinks about pricing**.

It intentionally does **not** define subscription plans, credit amounts, or prices.

Those evolve over time.

The philosophy behind them should remain stable.

Pricing should reinforce Kendrix's mission—not distort it.

---

## Pricing Philosophy

### We Charge for Value, Not AI

Creators are not purchasing:

* GPT requests
* image generations
* video renders
* tokens
* compute time

Creators are purchasing:

* better stories
* a better workflow
* saved time
* creative confidence
* consistent publishing

AI is an implementation detail.

Pricing should reflect creator outcomes—not technical infrastructure.

### Earn the Right to Charge

Revenue is a consequence of value.

Before optimizing monetization, Kendrix must consistently help creators produce stories they are proud to publish.

Charging too early damages trust.

Charging after clear value strengthens it.

### Sustainable Over Cheap

The goal is not to become the cheapest AI tool.

The goal is to become the most valuable storytelling workflow.

A creator who consistently publishes better stories receives more value than the cost of generation alone.

Pricing should reflect that.

### Transparent Pricing

Creators should understand:

* what they are paying for
* what consumes credits
* why certain actions cost more
* how to estimate usage

Avoid hidden costs.

Avoid surprise charges.

Transparency builds long-term trust.

### Predictability Matters

Creators should be able to estimate the cost of producing a story before they begin.

Pricing should reduce uncertainty rather than create it.

Where practical, estimate usage before generation.

### Simplicity Wins

Pricing should be easy to understand.

Avoid unnecessary complexity.

Avoid confusing feature matrices.

Avoid artificial restrictions that do not create meaningful customer value.

## Credit Philosophy

Credits exist to represent expensive production work.

Credits are **not** the product.

The product is storytelling.

Credits are simply a fair mechanism for allocating computational resources.

### Credits Should Match Creator Expectations

Whenever possible, creators should think in terms of stories rather than API calls.

Examples:

Good:

* approximately 5 stories
* approximately 20 renders

Poor:

* 14,283 AI tokens
* 67 GPU minutes

Translate infrastructure into creator language.

### Credit Costs Should Reflect Real Cost

Not every operation costs the same.

Credit consumption should broadly reflect underlying production cost while remaining easy for creators to understand.

Perfect precision is unnecessary.

Predictability is more valuable than mathematical accuracy.

Kendrix measures the real cost of every story as it is produced. Credit design derives from measured unit economics — never from estimates or competitor anchoring.

### Reward Efficiency

As Kendrix becomes more efficient, creators should benefit.

Model improvements, infrastructure optimizations, and workflow efficiencies should increase customer value before increasing company margin.

Credits never justify waste. An inefficiency is not acceptable because creators pay for it.

---

## Free Experience

The free experience should allow creators to understand Kendrix's value.

It should never feel intentionally broken.

Creators should be able to:

* experience the workflow
* understand the product
* complete meaningful work

The free plan is for learning—not exploitation.

---

## Paid Experience

Creators should pay because they have outgrown the free experience.

Not because essential functionality has been removed.

Paid plans should provide:

* higher production capacity
* greater convenience
* increased productivity
* advanced workflow capabilities

Never create artificial friction simply to encourage upgrades.

---

## What Is Never Tiered

The storytelling engine is never tiered.

Every creator — free or paid — gets Kendrix's best story: the same writer, the same craft, the same quality gates.

What scales with payment is capacity, convenience, production richness, and volume — never the quality of the story itself.

If a plan makes the story worse, the plan is wrong.

---

## AI Cost Philosophy

AI costs matter.

Creator value matters more.

The correct response to rising AI costs is usually:

* improve efficiency
* optimize workflows
* negotiate providers
* reduce waste

Not:

* degrade quality
* confuse pricing
* hide costs from creators

---

## Profitability

Profitability enables long-term product investment.

Healthy margins allow Kendrix to:

* improve quality
* support creators
* invest in research
* remain sustainable

Profit is a requirement for longevity—not the primary purpose of the company.

---

## Discounts

Discounts should reward meaningful relationships.

Examples:

* early supporters
* educational programs
* long-term customers

Avoid constant promotional pricing.

Creators should trust the value of the product.

---

## Enterprise Pricing

Enterprise pricing should reflect:

* operational requirements
* support expectations
* compliance needs
* organizational complexity

Enterprise features should not dictate pricing for independent creators.

---

## Pricing Evolution

Pricing should evolve with evidence.

Possible reasons to revisit pricing include:

* significant infrastructure changes
* creator feedback
* usage patterns
* new workflow capabilities
* improved production efficiency

Pricing should not change simply because competitors changed theirs.

---

## Relationship to the Roadmap

Pricing follows product maturity.

Foundation and Customer Zero phases focus on learning.

Private Alpha validates creator value.

Private Beta validates willingness to pay.

Public Launch optimizes sustainable growth.

Pricing should evolve alongside proof—not ahead of it.

---

## Relationship to Metrics

Pricing should improve:

* creator retention
* creator satisfaction
* sustainable revenue
* long-term trust

Revenue alone is not evidence of pricing success.

Healthy pricing produces healthy customers.

---

## What We Will Never Do

We will not:

* charge for unfinished value
* hide pricing
* surprise creators with unexpected costs
* optimize pricing at the expense of trust
* intentionally cripple the free experience
* use deceptive upgrade tactics
* prioritize short-term revenue over long-term relationships

---

## Review Questions

When evaluating pricing, ask:

* Are creators paying for meaningful value?
* Is pricing easy to understand?
* Does pricing encourage better storytelling?
* Does pricing support long-term sustainability?
* Has evidence justified this pricing decision?
* Would we feel comfortable explaining this pricing publicly?

---

## Definition of Success

Pricing succeeds when creators say:

"Kendrix is worth paying for because it helps me tell better stories."

Not:

"Kendrix has the cheapest AI credits."

If creators compare us only on generation cost, we have failed to communicate our true value.

Pricing should reinforce what Kendrix actually sells:

A better way to create stories.
