## Purpose

This roadmap defines how Kendrix progresses from an idea into a sustainable business.

It is **not** a feature backlog.

It is a proof roadmap.

Every phase exists to answer a specific question.

Kendrix only advances when the previous phase has been validated.

Features do not determine progress.

Evidence does.

The execution backlog lives at /ROADMAP.md. This document governs permission; that one governs execution. They should never become the same document.

---

## Roadmap Philosophy

We do not build everything at once.

We deliberately delay ideas until they solve a proven problem.

Every phase should reduce uncertainty.

Progress is measured by learning—not by shipping features.

---

## Roadmap Principles

### Build the next company, not the final company.

Each phase should prepare the next phase.

Avoid building infrastructure for users who do not yet exist.

### Build Foundations Early

Most features should wait until evidence justifies them.

However, architectural foundations that are expensive or risky to retrofit should be implemented correctly from the beginning.

Examples include:

* tenant isolation
* authorization boundaries
* per-story cost accounting
* auditability
* core data ownership

Delaying features is discipline.

Delaying foundations is technical debt.

### Evidence before expansion.

Do not expand into new audiences, products, or workflows until the current one demonstrates meaningful success.

### One bottleneck at a time.

Every phase has one primary objective.

If multiple priorities compete, identify the current bottleneck and solve it first.

### Delay complexity.

Teams.

Marketplace.

Enterprise.

Advanced collaboration.

These are valuable ideas—but only after they solve a real problem for active creators.

## Phase 0 — Foundation

### Goal

Build the storytelling engine.

### Primary Question

Can Kendrix consistently produce high-quality story-driven videos?

### Focus

* Core workflow
* Story generation
* Storyboarding
* Visual generation
* Narration
* Audio
* Assembly
* Publishing pipeline

### Customer

Internal only.

### Success Looks Like

* End-to-end workflow functions reliably.
* Story quality meets editorial expectations.
* The production pipeline is stable.

### Exit Criteria

* Customer Zero can consistently produce publishable content.
* Critical production blockers have been resolved.
* Internal confidence is high.

---

## Phase 1 — Customer Zero

### Goal

Use Kendrix to build Kendrix.

### Primary Question

Would we genuinely choose Kendrix over our previous workflow?

### Focus

Operate our own creator channels.

Examples:

* Horror
* Children's Stories
* Educational
* Documentary

### Customer

The founder.

### Success Looks Like

* Content is published consistently.
* Workflow becomes faster through iteration.
* Quality improves over time.
* Product friction is identified and reduced.

### Exit Criteria

* Kendrix becomes the default workflow for our own channels.
* We would continue using Kendrix even if no external users existed yet.
* We can confidently demonstrate the product to others.
* Customer Zero provides external evidence that the workflow produces content audiences engage with. Evidence may include:
  * audience retention
  * comments
  * repeat viewers
  * shares
  * organic growth

Internal confidence alone is insufficient.

## Phase 2 — Private Alpha

### Goal

Validate with a small group of trusted creators.

### Primary Question

Can creators successfully use Kendrix without the founder guiding every step?

### Focus

* Onboarding
* Usability
* Workflow clarity
* Stability
* Creator interviews

### Customer

Invite-only creators, personally invited by the founder.

Quality matters more than quantity.

### Success Looks Like

* Creators complete projects independently.
* Feedback identifies recurring patterns rather than isolated issues.
* Creators voluntarily return to build additional stories.

### Exit Criteria

* High creator satisfaction.
* Strong retention among alpha creators.
* Clear evidence that Kendrix solves a meaningful problem.

---

## Phase 3 — Private Beta

### Goal

Prove repeatable value.

### Primary Question

Do creators continue using Kendrix because it improves their storytelling workflow?

### Focus

* Retention
* Reliability
* Scalability
* Editorial refinement

Marketing-driven recruitment begins when this phase starts.

### Success Looks Like

* Consistent weekly usage.
* Organic referrals.
* Positive creator testimonials.
* Repeat publishing.

### Exit Criteria

* Strong retention.
* Product reliability.
* Sustainable operating costs.

## Phase 4 — Public Launch

### Goal

Open Kendrix to a broader audience.

### Primary Question

Can the product grow while maintaining quality?

### Focus

* Self-service onboarding
* Billing
* Support
* Documentation
* Performance

* Healthy creator growth.
* Stable onboarding.
* Sustainable revenue.
* Positive customer experience.

### Exit Criteria

* Operational stability.
* Predictable growth.

## Phase 5 — Growth

### Goal

Scale thoughtfully.

### Primary Question

Which investments create the greatest long-term value?

Potential areas include:

* Teams
* Collaboration
* Marketplace
* API
* Integrations
* Enterprise capabilities

These are opportunities—not commitments.

Every expansion must be justified by evidence from existing creators.

---

## Decision Gates

A phase should not advance because:

* it took a long time
* the roadmap says so
* competitors shipped something
* exciting ideas exist

A phase advances only when its exit criteria have been satisfied.

---

## Things We Will Intentionally Delay

Until evidence exists, we will avoid prioritizing:

* Teams
* Enterprise features
* Marketplace
* Plugin ecosystem
* White-labeling
* Advanced permissions
* Complex billing
* Multi-tenant organizations
* AI model selection
* Extensive customization

These ideas remain in the opportunity backlog until validated by creator demand.

Delaying these features never means delaying foundations — see Build Foundations Early. Tenant isolation and cost accounting are foundations, not features.

---

## Customer Zero

Customer Zero is Kendrix itself.

Our own channels are the first proof that the product works.

Before asking creators to trust Kendrix, we should trust it ourselves.

Customer Zero provides:

* product validation
* marketing content
* tutorials
* onboarding examples
* case studies
* quality benchmarks

Every improvement should first benefit Customer Zero.

---

## Relationship to Marketing

Marketing follows the roadmap.

During Foundation and Customer Zero phases, marketing focuses on:

* building in public
* educating creators
* sharing product progress
* establishing trust

At Private Alpha, the founder personally invites trusted creators.

Marketing-driven recruitment begins at Private Beta.

---

## Relationship to Pricing

Pricing follows validation.

Revenue optimization is not the priority before creators consistently receive value.

The product should earn the right to charge before optimizing pricing strategy.

---

## Relationship to Metrics

Success metrics differ by phase.

Early phases measure learning.

Later phases measure growth.

Metrics should always reflect the current stage of the roadmap.

---

## Review Questions

At the end of every month, ask:

* What assumptions were validated?
* What assumptions were disproven?
* What did creators teach us?
* Has the primary bottleneck changed?
* Are we solving today's problem—or tomorrow's?
* Does the next phase still make sense?

Update the roadmap only when evidence justifies a change.

---

## Definition of Success

The roadmap succeeds when Kendrix grows because creators consistently receive meaningful value—not because we shipped the most features.

Our goal is not to reach Phase 5 quickly.

Our goal is to reach every phase with confidence.
