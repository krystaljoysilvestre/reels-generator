# Decision Framework

This framework should be used whenever evaluating new features, roadmap ideas, marketing initiatives, pricing changes, or technical investments.

Every recommendation should follow these steps.

---

## Step 1 — Does it solve a real creator problem?

Questions:

* What problem does this solve?
* How painful is that problem?
* Who experiences it?
* How often does it occur?

If the problem is unclear, stop.

Do not build solutions searching for problems.

Decide from observed problems, not predicted ones. If the problem is a prediction, gather evidence before building — a hypothesis is not a backlog item. Record the prediction in company/assumptions.md and test it.

---

## Step 2 — Does it strengthen Kendrix's positioning?

Ask:

Does this move Kendrix closer to becoming the best storytelling operating system?

Or does it make Kendrix feel like another generic AI tool?

Prefer differentiation over feature parity.

---

## Step 3 — Will creators notice?

Many technically impressive features create little user value.

Ask:

Would creators actually notice this improvement?

Would they care?

Would they mention it to another creator?

If not, reconsider the priority.

---

## Step 4 — Is it the highest leverage use of a solo founder's time?

Engineering time is limited.

Ask:

Is this the best use of this week's development effort?

Could a simpler solution provide most of the value?

Avoid perfection when iteration is sufficient.

Weigh reversibility. A decision you can undo cheaply deserves speed and iteration; one you can't deserves evidence and review first.

---

## Step 5 — What does it cost?

Evaluate:

* Engineering effort
* AI costs
* Infrastructure costs
* Maintenance
* Customer support
* Long-term complexity

Always consider total cost—not just implementation.

---

## Step 6 — Does it simplify or complicate the product?

Every feature adds:

* UI
* documentation
* testing
* support
* maintenance
* cognitive load

Prefer removing complexity over adding capability.

---

## Step 7 — Is now the right time?

Categorize every idea as:

NOW

Supports the current product vision and unlocks immediate value.

NEXT

Important, but dependent on earlier work.

LATER

Valuable after product-market fit.

NEVER

Interesting, but not aligned with Kendrix's mission.

The strategy roadmap (company/roadmap.md) defines what proof unlocks NEXT and LATER. Phases advance on proof, not dates.

---

## Final Question

If this feature disappeared tomorrow...

Would creators miss it?

If the answer is no, think carefully before building it.

---

## Guiding Principle

The goal is not to build the most features.

The goal is to build the strongest storytelling product.

Every decision should reinforce that mission.

When principles conflict, the Core Philosophy ordering in START-HERE.md decides.
