## Purpose

This document defines how Kendrix measures success.

Metrics exist to answer one question:

**Are we making meaningful progress toward our mission?**

Metrics should reinforce the roadmap—not replace it.

Every phase of the roadmap has different success criteria.

Optimizing the wrong metric at the wrong time is a strategic mistake.

---

## Measurement Philosophy

### Measure Outcomes, Not Activity

Building more features is not success.

Generating more AI content is not success.

Real progress is measured by creator outcomes.

### Metrics Follow the Roadmap

Success changes as Kendrix matures.

Early phases optimize learning.

Later phases optimize sustainable growth.

Never judge one phase using another phase's metrics.

### Quality Before Quantity

Publishing one memorable story is more valuable than producing ten forgettable ones.

A smaller number of successful creators is more valuable than a large number of inactive accounts.

### Leading Indicators Over Vanity Metrics

Prefer measurements that predict long-term success.

Examples:

* creator retention
* stories published
* creator satisfaction
* referrals
* workflow completion

Avoid optimizing numbers that create little customer value.

### Metrics Can Be Gamed

Any metric, once targeted, invites the behavior that inflates it instead of the outcome it stood for. We have measured this internally: a quality gate that rewarded punchy short lines produced machine monoculture, not better stories.

Every primary metric is paired with a counter-metric that catches its gaming: pass rate with quality spot-audits, retention with satisfaction, revenue with retention.

When a metric improves suspiciously fast, audit the behavior before celebrating the number.

### Evidence Before Assumptions

Metrics should validate decisions—not justify them.

If the data contradicts our assumptions, we change the assumptions.

---

## Anti-Metrics

The following should never become primary success metrics.

They may be monitored but should not drive decisions.

Examples:

* registered accounts
* page views
* feature count
* AI generations
* API requests
* lines of code
* social media impressions
* downloads

These numbers are useful only when connected to creator value.

## Phase Metrics

### Foundation

Primary Goal

Build a reliable storytelling system.

Success Looks Like

* publishable stories
* stable production pipeline
* reliable generation
* acceptable generation cost
* editorial quality (measured as the story gate's first-attempt pass rate)

Not Success

* revenue
* signups
* paid conversions

Review Questions

* Can Customer Zero consistently produce high-quality stories?
* Is the production workflow dependable?
* Are quality gates passing?

### Customer Zero

Primary Goal

Validate the workflow through our own creator channels.

Success Looks Like

* consistent publishing
* workflow improvements over time
* reduced production friction
* audience engagement
* audience retention
* repeatable production

Not Success

* going viral
* subscriber count
* sponsorships

Review Questions

* Would we continue using Kendrix ourselves?
* Are our stories improving?
* Are audiences engaging with the content?
* What does Customer Zero teach us every week?

### Private Alpha

Primary Goal

Validate creator value.

Success Looks Like

* creator activation
* creator retention
* successful onboarding
* repeat projects
* qualitative feedback

Not Success

* large user counts
* rapid growth

Review Questions

* Can creators succeed without founder guidance?
* Do they voluntarily return?
* What friction appears repeatedly?

### Private Beta

Primary Goal

Validate repeatable value.

Success Looks Like

* weekly active creators
* retention
* referrals
* willingness to pay
* creator satisfaction

Not Success

* maximizing revenue
* aggressive acquisition

Review Questions

* Are creators recommending Kendrix?
* Do they continue publishing?
* Are they willing to pay because they see value?

### Public Launch

Primary Goal

Build a sustainable business.

Success Looks Like

* healthy retention
* sustainable revenue
* customer satisfaction
* predictable growth
* operational stability

Review Questions

* Are we growing responsibly?
* Are creators succeeding?
* Are operations sustainable?

### Growth

Growth-phase metrics are defined when evidence defines the phase — its opportunities are not yet commitments.

## Expected Metric Impact

Every significant proposal should estimate its expected impact.

Example:

Expected Impact

Positive

* creator retention
* workflow completion

Neutral

* revenue

Negative

* engineering complexity

Recommendations without measurable impact should be challenged.

---

## Metric Hierarchy

When metrics conflict, prioritize them in this order:

1. Creator Success
2. Story Quality
3. Product Reliability
4. Sustainable Business
5. Growth

Growth should never come at the expense of creator trust or storytelling quality.

(This is the metrics-domain instance of the Core Philosophy ordering in START-HERE.md — the same law, so the two rankings must never drift apart.)

---

## Relationship to Pricing

Pricing exists to support creator success.

Revenue is healthy only if creators continue receiving meaningful value.

Short-term pricing gains that reduce long-term retention are failures.

---

## Relationship to Marketing

Marketing succeeds when it attracts creators who become successful with Kendrix.

Traffic without successful creators is not meaningful growth.

---

## Relationship to Product

Product decisions should improve one or more meaningful metrics.

If a feature has no measurable impact, reconsider whether it should be built.

---

## Review Questions

At the end of each month, ask:

* Which metrics improved?
* Which metrics declined?
* What changed?
* Which assumptions were incorrect?
* Has the primary bottleneck shifted?
* Are we still measuring the right things for this phase?

## Definition of Success

Metrics succeed when they help Kendrix make better decisions.

The purpose of measurement is not to produce impressive numbers.

The purpose of measurement is to improve creator outcomes.

If a metric encourages behavior that harms creators or weakens storytelling, the metric—not the creator—is wrong.
