> The pipeline knows two narrator perspectives — `first_person` and `third_person` ([story-bible.ts:76](../packages/core/src/types/story-bible.ts#L76)) — and both are **diegetic**: the narrator is a person inside the story's world who can be shown. A news explainer, documentary, video essay, or listicle is narrated **non-diegetically**: the voice sits *above* the world and is never in frame. That concept does not exist anywhere in the system, so the pipeline manufactures a body for a voice that doesn't have one. Call the missing value **`voiceover`**.

## The failure this explains (2026-07-24, production, $0.48 of discarded storyboard)

A factual Taglish news explainer (voiceover narration, "dito sa atin… minerals natin… Ikaw?") run through the standard `analyze_story → breakdown_scenes → generate_images` path produced a 12-scene storyboard in which:

- The analyzer extracted a **narrator character entity** and placed him in the journey legs — as its prompt *mandates*: "THE FIRST-PERSON NARRATOR IS ALWAYS AN ENTITY … you MUST extract the narrator as a character entity with a FULLY committed look" ([story-analyzer.v1.ts:55](../packages/core/src/prompts/story-analyzer.v1.ts#L55)). Taglish first-person-plural news register ("sa atin", "natin") pattern-matches the first-person cues (:77).
- Breakdown then rendered the invented host across ≥5 scenes (a man on a rooftop, at a desk, shaking hands), POV framings ("the narrator's hand rests on a railing"), and disembodied floating hands — the POV_RULE machinery ([scenes.v1.ts:152-159](../packages/core/src/prompts/scenes.v1.ts#L152)) plus the narrator-visibility splice ([generate-images.ts:255-276](../apps/worker/src/pipeline/steps/generate-images.ts#L255)) doing exactly what they were built to do, for the wrong kind of narrator.
- Worst single output: **"Close-up of DTI officials shaking hands with an enthusiastic narrator. Flags of the Philippines and the US…"** — rendered photoreal, it reads as a **press photo of real officials at a real event that never happened**. (See the companion floor below.)
- `scene-qa` flagged three static-person risks and an over-cut, but had **no concept** that this reel shouldn't have a host at all — QA validates against the same wrong assumption, so the failure is invisible to every downstream check.

The control that proves the gap is the *concept*, not the model: the same run, re-boarded by hand as 9 faceless b-roll scenes (concept renders, maps, macro, landscape) with the same style preset, rendered **first-try clean — zero re-rolls, zero host, zero ethics violations**. The model executes faceless b-roll perfectly when something tells it to. Nothing in the system can tell it.

## Why this is a missing responsibility, not a prompt bug

- **The genre module can't fix it.** `scenes.v1`'s informational module already says "show the actual thing being explained" ([scenes.v1.ts:119](../packages/core/src/prompts/scenes.v1.ts#L119)) — and was overridden anyway, because the narrator entity arrives *upstream* in the bible and the journey legs place him in every leg. Extraction outranks genre guidance; the fix must land at extraction level.
- **Story Modes can't own it.** Mode (`standard`/`ebn`) is the *generation discipline* (how text earns truth); presentation is *who appears on screen*. An EBN documentary and a standard-mode news recap are different modes with the same voiceover presentation; a hosted explainer and a faceless one are the same mode with different presentations. Orthogonal axes.
- **It is not genre-conditional.** Voiceover is the native register of most informational content, but a hosted informational channel (a talking-head explainer) is legitimate, and a faceless horror-compilation channel narrates fiction as voiceover. Hardcoding `if genreCategory === 'informational' → faceless` would be exactly the kind of single-texture genre default the unbiased-design rule forbids. The axis must be its own signal.

## The insight — one enum value, not a new system

The smallest mechanism that carries the whole fix: **add `voiceover` to `narratorPerspective`** and let the existing seams key off it.

**What this field really is (naming honesty, per editorial review):** the axis being extended is **embodiment/presentation**, not grammatical person. Narration has two dimensions the current field conflates — *who says "I"* (grammatical person) and *does the teller have a body in the story's world* (diegesis). Every current consumer of `narratorPerspective` (the scenes perspective rule, the narrator-visibility splice, `narratorEntityId` nullability) consumes only the embodiment axis; the three values are really three **visual grammars** — camera-as-eyes, camera-as-observer, camera-as-illustrator — wearing grammatical-person names. A first-person video *essay* ("I think this policy fails…") is first-person in voice and `voiceover` on screen, and no consumer loses information.

1. **Analyzer** ([story-analyzer.v1](../packages/core/src/prompts/story-analyzer.v1.ts)): may emit `narratorPerspective: 'voiceover'` — narrator is NOT an entity, `narratorEntityId` stays null, `present` lists only people the narration itself depicts. The "narrator is ALWAYS an entity" mandate becomes conditional on the perspective being diegetic. **The tiebreak is the participant test: did the teller LIVE the events (diegetic — POV stands, even first-person, even on a faceless channel), or is the teller REPORTING/ARGUING about events (voiceover)? Pronouns are evidence; participation is the verdict.** First-person *cues* ("natin", "I think") no longer force embodiment — commentary voice ≠ character.
2. **Channel context, not channel command:** the channel passes its presentation identity as *context* the analyzer reasons from (News Today PH: "narration is voiceover commentary; no host appears on screen"), the same way genre and tone are passed today. Per-story judgment stays with the analyzer — a channel hint biases, a story's actual text decides; the participant test is what keeps the hint from steamrolling a lived first-person story into wallpaper. (Owner-set, overridable — the legitimate tuning exception; never `if genre`.)
3. **Scenes** ([scenes.v1.ts:152-159](../packages/core/src/prompts/scenes.v1.ts#L152)): a third perspective rule beside POV_RULE/OBSERVER_RULE — b-roll grammar, judgment-framed but **armed with a teaching goal and a named failure mode** (the axis is genre-agnostic while the deep teaching grammar lives only in the informational module, so the rule must carry its own floor): *goal — every shot answers "what is the narration claiming right now"; a sound-off viewer should still follow the argument's objects. Failure mode — wallpaper: a shot that would board equally well under any other script on this topic. Show the subject of the narration, never the speaker; people appear only when the narration's content itself places them, framed per the existing CAST CONTROL / static-pose rules; environment, object, concept, and data-visualization shots are all valid; any beat may be people-free.* No quotas, no forced shot types.
4. **Image assembly** ([generate-images.ts:255-276](../apps/worker/src/pipeline/steps/generate-images.ts#L255)): `voiceover` short-circuits the narrator-visibility splice entirely (nothing to splice — there is no narrator body). Existing code path, one more branch of an existing conditional.
5. **Journey semantics under voiceover (defined, not hand-waved):** the journey schema is protagonist-shaped (`occupancyState`, movement-is-continuous, transition validation — [story-analyzer.v1.ts:88](../packages/core/src/prompts/story-analyzer.v1.ts#L88), [story-bible.ts:64](../packages/core/src/types/story-bible.ts#L64)), and an explainer legitimately "teleports" (Palawan mine → DTI office → world market is argument, not locomotion). Under `voiceover`, journey legs become a **subject-matter itinerary**: one leg per contiguous stretch of the argument's *setting*, keeping `locationEntityId`/`present` (so a recurring real place still renders consistently) while occupancy/movement/continuity rules are suspended. Breakdown's "obey EXACTLY" then points at a defined structure, and location-entity consistency — the bible's core benefit — is preserved.
6. **Perspective-aware `scene-qa` (Phase 1, non-optional):** the 2026-07-24 failure was invisible to every downstream check because QA validates against the same wrong assumption. Under `voiceover`, scene-qa flags speaker/host/POV framings as defects — closing the blindness this doc itself diagnosed. (Flagged for Phase 1, related: QA *targeting* equates risk with person-presence ([generate-images.ts:241-253](../apps/worker/src/pipeline/steps/generate-images.ts#L241)), so a voiceover reel's load-bearing teaching frames currently escape QA; the target heuristic should key on teaching load for voiceover reels.)

Everything downstream (timings, TTS, music, render) is untouched — the voice was always non-diegetic in the *audio*; this makes the *visuals* agree.

**Expected evolution (recorded so a future author extends rather than replaces):** the nearest format that breaks the reel-wide enum is the **hosted explainer with b-roll cutaways** — presentation changes per *beat* (host direct-address on some spans, voiceover on others), and direct address is currently *anti-modeled* (the static camera-facing pose is treated as a defect by scenes rules and QA). When that format is committed, the reel-wide value becomes the default and journey legs grow a per-leg presentation override — an extension of this design, not a rewrite.

## Companion floor (separable — ships independently): depictive ethics for real parties

The officials-handshake failure is not a perspective bug — with a `voiceover` perspective the narrator vanishes but "DTI officials shaking hands" could still render. Factual narration names real living people, offices, and organizations; today **no layer prevents rendering them as photoreal event imagery** (fabricated press photos). EBN's illustrative profile covers `storyMode='ebn'` only; [depictive-fidelity](../specs/depictive-fidelity.md)'s three-tier people stance (OFF for living people) governs reference *sourcing*, not generated *depiction*; image-QA checks brief-match, not ethics.

Floor (hard constraint — safety, so firmly encoded, per unbiased-design): **in photoreal output, never depict an identifiable real living person, or a named real organization's officials/events, as if photographed; render representative, anonymous, or conceptual imagery instead** (distant silhouettes, hands-free wides, symbolic staging — the hand-boarded run's scene 7 is the pattern). This is a **rendering-profile floor** under the visual-director's floors-are-law charter, shipping on its own timeline, likely *before* the perspective work. **Drafted in full (wording, placement inventory, boundaries, edge cases) at [specs/depictive-ethics-floor.md](../specs/depictive-ethics-floor.md) — awaiting founder ratification.**

## Non-goals

- **Not a fiction change.** First-person horror POV stays exactly as built — POV is *correct* for diegetic narrators. `voiceover` adds a value; it modifies nothing about the other two.
- **Not grammatical person.** This field never encodes who says "I" — it encodes embodiment. If a future consumer needs grammatical person as such, that is a separate signal, not a fourth reading of this enum.
- **Not an on-screen host.** `voiceover` must never be stretched to cover a direct-address presenter — that grammar is currently *anti-modeled* (camera-facing static pose is the system's #1 flagged failure, scenes.v1.ts:213, image-qa). A hosted format earns its own value (`presenter`) with its own scene grammar and QA inversions; stretching `voiceover` there would destroy the enum's meaning.
- **Not the EBN illustrative floor or disclosure** — that stays welded to `storyMode='ebn'` (see [evidence-bible.md](evidence-bible.md) / specs/evidence-based-narrative.md).
- **Not caption/on-screen-text policy, not TTS/voice anything.**
- **Not a general shot-grammar rework** and **not a "no people" rule** — voiceover b-roll may show people when the content calls for them; the floor above governs *which* people and *how*.
- **Not auto-detection of channel identity** — the channel hint is owner-set DNA (wizard-derived default, editable), never inferred silently at render time.

## Strongest known objections

1. **"n=1 channel, and it's the owner's personal side-project — is this Kendrix's problem?"** The evidence run is n=1, honestly. But the axis is format-level, not channel-level: news, documentary, educational, finance, listicle — the informational family's native register is voiceover, and **EBN documentary is already the committed lead specialization**; its narration will hit this identical wall at render. The second consumer is already on the roadmap.
2. **"Couldn't the analyzer just get smarter about first-person cues?"** Better cue-reading fixes the *misclassification* (Taglish "natin" ≠ "ako") but not the *missing value* — a correctly-classified news script still has no honest perspective to be classified *as*. Two diegetic options force a lie either way: `first_person` invents a host; `third_person` still journeys a narrator through the world.
3. **"Is one production run enough to add a schema value?"** The counter-weight is asymmetry of cost: one enum value + conditional prompt language, against an entire content family being unusable on the auto path (12/12 scenes discarded, $0.48 sunk, full hand re-board required). And the Phase 0 probe is designed to kill it cheaply if the mechanism doesn't hold.

## Falsifiability

- **Phase 0 fails on (1)** — prompt-level context can't stop host insertion → the mechanism is wrong (extraction can't be steered by context alone); retire this mechanism and design a structural bypass (a voiceover path that skips narrator machinery entirely).
- **Phase 0 fails on (2) or (3)** — fiction regresses, or a channel hint steamrolls a lived first-person story → the conditional language / participant test leaks; fix or retire. (2) is a hard gate.
- **Phase 0 fails on (4)** — boards go generic-wallpaper → the gap is in *teaching grammar*, not the perspective axis; narrow the claim. Known concentration of this risk: channels outside the informational module — `general` is an empty module ([scenes.v1.ts:127](../packages/core/src/prompts/scenes.v1.ts#L127)), so voiceover there leans entirely on the perspective rule's own teaching hook. If wallpaper appears mainly in the `general` bucket, the fix is module depth, not the axis.
- **The falsifying product** (recorded per review): a **hosted talking-head explainer** — it forces a fourth enum value (`presenter`, direct address) and inverts the system's most-enforced rule (camera-facing = failure). If the axis survives that by *growing a value* rather than stretching `voiceover`, it's real architecture; if not, this was "faceless mode" wearing an enum. A reaction-format channel breaks the reel-wide granularity assumption second (perspective per leg, not per reel).
- **Adoption threshold:** if no voiceover-format product (news channel, EBN documentary) is committed within the horizon of the EBN rollout, this stays a hypothesis indefinitely — the doc carries the knowledge.
